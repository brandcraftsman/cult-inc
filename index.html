EXHIBIT A
01 / Foundational Claims

Foundational and Historical Truth Claims

Where the church's own founding narrative meets archaeology, genetics, and the historical record — and where critics say the two stop lining up.

UNVERIFIED
  1. A–1

    Book of Mormon Anachronisms

    Archaeological, linguistic, and biological inconsistencies — including references to steel, horses, and wheeled chariots in a pre-Columbian American setting where no supporting evidence for any of them has been found.

  2. A–2

    DNA Evidence

    Genetic studies on Native American populations contradict the narrative that indigenous Americans are primarily descendants of ancient Israelites, showing East Asian and Siberian genetic markers instead.

  3. A–3

    Nineteenth-Century Milieu

    Historians argue the text reflects 19th-century frontier themes — such as the Mound Builder myth and anti-Masonry — rather than an ancient record.

  4. A–4

    Translation Method

    Historical records and church essays confirm Joseph Smith translated using a seer stone in a hat rather than physical observation of the plates using the Urim and Thummim.

  5. A–5

    The Book of Abraham

    Papyri claimed to be translated by Smith into the Book of Abraham were later identified by Egyptologists as standard Egyptian funerary texts — the Book of Breathings.

  6. A–6

    Shifting First Vision Accounts

    The earliest written account of the First Vision (1835) does not feature both God the Father and Jesus Christ, pointing to evolving theology over time.

EXHIBIT B
02 / Institutional Record

Institutional Evolution and Doctrines of Exclusion

Policies the church has since reversed, revised, or continues to defend — and the people they were built around excluding.

DOCUMENTED
  1. B–1

    Priesthood and Temple Ban

    Black men were barred from holding the priesthood, and members were barred from temple ordinances, until 1978 — with past justifications later labeled as folklore.

  2. B–2

    Polygamy and Polyandry

    Historical records reveal polyandry (marrying women already married to other living men), the marriage of teenage girls, and past public denials of the practice.

  3. B–3

    Gender Hierarchies

    Administrative and spiritual authority remains strictly reserved for men, creating a complementarian structure that critics view as patriarchal.

Institutional policy shapes lived experience. Exhibit C follows the trail from doctrine to the psychological toll critics attribute to it.

EXHIBIT C
03 / Lived Experience

Psychological and Social Impact

How belief structures are said to shape doubt, identity, and family life for members and former members alike.

UNRESOLVED
  1. C–1

    Epistemological Confinement

    Relying primarily on spiritual confirmation creates circular logic, where doubts or historical anomalies are attributed to personal spiritual failure.

  2. C–2

    LGBTQ+ Policies

    Strict stances on gender and marriage have caused severe friction, family division, and psychological trauma among queer individuals.

  3. C–3

    High-Demand Control (BITE Model)

    Critics apply control models to aspects like temple garments, mandatory tithing, discouragement of critical sources, and strict lifestyle rules.

EXHIBIT D
04 / The Ledger

Financial Transparency and Corporate Structure

What's known — and what isn't — about how the church manages money given by its members.

UNDISCLOSED
  1. D–1

    Massive Wealth Accumulation

    Whistleblower reports highlighted an investment fund exceeding $100 billion, leading to criticism over the balance between wealth hoarding and humanitarian aid.

  2. D–2

    Lack of Transparency

    A centralized hierarchy contrasts with local volunteer labor, with little transparency regarding tithing funds and internal institutional handling of issues.

This dossier collects objections as they are commonly raised by historians, former members, and researchers — it is a summary of arguments, not a verdict on them.
